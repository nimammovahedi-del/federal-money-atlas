Three cleaner ways to show one answer.

The last version had too much on every screen and no clear place to start. Each direction below rebuilds every page — map, county, list, compare and about — in the style of nader.io: light gray, near-black type, Inter, and one signal green.

The one question

$1 in,
$? back.

For every $1 a county's residents pay in federal taxes, how much federal money comes back?

Color

Green = money that came back

Black = taxes paid. Everything else is gray. Green is never used for rankings or decoration.

Type

Inter + JetBrains Mono

Heavy, tightly spaced headlines; small spaced-out labels; mono only for figures.

Kept

Land · People · Grid, filters, neighbors

The itemized table became three headline numbers and a CSV download.

Checked

3 independent reviews

Usability, accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA) and visual design. Their fixes are already applied.

Fixed after review

  • Search finds the right county“Washington, OH”, “washington county ohio”, “saint louis” and misspellings like “mcdowl” now work; no-match offers the list.
  • One comparison, one formatCounty pages compare only with the typical county ($1.09); every figure is in dollars ($0.74, not 74¢).
  • Plain warnings for outliersCounties driven by one big contract, base or lab say so next to the number (e.g. Chattahoochee, GA: 15.5× the typical county).
  • No fake zerosKalawao, HI (taxes counted with Maui) shows “not reported” instead of $0.
  • Green means money in, onlyRemoved green from rank tiles, compare cards, chips and links.
  • Readable map colorsFive greens without a near-black step, each swatch labeled with its range, hatching for no data, white for counties outside the size filter.
  • Results next to the mapClicking a county or square opens a panel on the map with a close button; squares say how many counties match the filter.
  • A text version of the map“View this as a list” opens the list with the same money type and size filter, sorted.
  • Keyboard and screen readersFocus stays on filter buttons, visible focus rings everywhere, headings for every section, page titles per view, search results announced.
  • Layout fixesA: one focal point on the home page. B: map moved up, filters on one line. C: square corners, no shadows, plain nav with its own Map link.
  • Compare“Copy link” button, a consistent “Social Security per $1” measure, and B adds bars per row.
  • PhonesQuestion and search first; no sideways scrolling on any of the 15 pages at 375px.

Recommendation

A's grid, with C's way in.

The reviewers split: the design reviewer picked A because it actually looks like nader.io; the usability reviewer picked C because it's clearest where to start. The natural merge is A's hairline grid and county page, opening with C's single big search and “Try” examples — and B's calm list rows for the Counties page.

Not verified yet: real screen-reader testing (VoiceOver/NVDA), and tests with people who haven't seen the site. Connecticut is still missing from the map.

Built with real Phase 0 data (fiscal year 2023, 3,144 counties). Sources: USAspending.gov, IRS Statistics of Income, U.S. Treasury, Census Bureau. Design reference: nader.io.
