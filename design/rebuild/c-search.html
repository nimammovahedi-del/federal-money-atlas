Questions and answers

A prototype, not published. Fiscal year 2023 data.

What does “back per $1” mean?

Federal money that reached the county (Social Security & SSI, grants and contracts) divided by the federal taxes its residents paid, both for fiscal year 2023.

Typical county or U.S. overall?

The typical county ($1.09) is the one in the middle. The U.S. overall figure ($0.74) adds up every county; it's lower because a few high-income places pay much of the tax. Answers compare with the typical county.

Where do the numbers come from?

Money in: USAspending.gov. Taxes: IRS county data for income tax; payroll, corporate, estate and excise taxes are estimated and scaled to U.S. Treasury totals. Population: Census Bureau.

What's estimated?

Medicaid and other statewide grants have no county, so each county gets a population share. Taxes other than income tax are estimated too. Estimates are marked “est.”

Why is the U.S. figure under $1?

Medicare, veterans' payments, federal salaries, defense overhead and interest on the debt aren't tied to a county here.

What do Land, People and Grid mean?

Land shows counties at their real size. People draws each county as a circle sized by its residents. Grid combines nearby counties into equal squares. Connecticut reports 9 planning regions and isn't drawn yet.
