Federal Money Atlas

How much federal money comes back to each U.S. county for every $1 its residents pay in federal taxes.

Working name · Prototype v1 · Civic data · Free public website · Data: fiscal year 2023
View prototype →

The question

Federal money reaches every county as Social Security checks, grants and contracts, but no one publishes, county by county, how that compares with the federal taxes residents pay. The last widely cited county estimate is from 2004, and the Census Bureau's county spending report ended with fiscal year 2010.

The site answers one question per county, in one sentence, with every number traceable to a public source. It is a neutral reference: no ratings of places as winners or losers, no party colors.

Design decisions

A dashboard that asks questions

Borrowed from Axis Maps' California Birth Equity Connect. Panels beside the map are titled as questions ("What came back?", "Which counties?"). Each option shows its national figure. Choosing one recolors the map and rewrites the map's title, so the question on screen always matches what's drawn.

Three ways to see the map

Land draws counties at their real size. People redraws each county as a circle sized by its population, because most Americans live in small, dense counties that a normal map hides. Grid (also from Axis Maps) groups nearby counties into equal cells over a muted basemap with city labels, smoothing out tiny counties with extreme values.

The answer comes first

Every county page opens with a sentence: "For every $1 residents paid in federal taxes, $0.75 in federal money came back, less than the typical county." Then one ledger: what came in, the dollars-back number with the U.S. average and the typical county, and what residents paid, on one shared scale.

Details on demand

The itemized table, neighbors, notes, "not counted" items and the method sit below the answer or in folded sections. Nothing is removed; it's simply not competing with the answer.

A list for people who prefer tables

Also following Axis Maps: every county in a sortable, filterable table, with the same size groups as the map.

Compare, with a shareable link

Pick a measure, pin up to three counties, and the address bar always reproduces the view, so a reporter can send exactly what they see.

Estimates are always marked

Grants from statewide programs (like Medicaid) and most non-income taxes are estimated. They carry an "est." tag and striped bars wherever they appear.

Color and type

Green for money in (three shades: Social Security, grants, contracts) and umber for taxes, everywhere. Neither is a party color. Map colors split counties into six equal-sized groups so the map shows real differences. Barlow and Barlow Condensed throughout, on an atlas-like paper and charcoal palette.

How the numbers were checked

Phase 0 rebuilt the figures from public files and compared them with published totals. Social Security and SSI came within 0.3% of Treasury's benefit payments. Contracts matched USAspending's published $760B total. State tax estimates landed within 8% of the Rockefeller Institute's state study (0.6% overall), and state results correlate at 0.97 with it.

What's next

Share statewide programs by enrollment instead of population; move nationwide contracts booked at one office into a "national programs" line; add five years of history and the monthly list of new awards and cancellations; a full methods page; and a newer base map that includes Connecticut's planning regions.
